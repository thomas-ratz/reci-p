## Method

### 1. Bake the coffee crumble

1. Blend the **100 g hazelnuts** to a fine powder.
2. Transfer to a bowl and add the sugar, flour, butter, flaky salt, instant coffee and melted dark chocolate.
3. Knead until it's a soft, even dough.
4. Spread it on a tray lined with a silicone sheet.
5. Bake at **160°C** for around **25 minutes**, stirring **every 5 minutes** to create a fine crumble.
6. Cool **completely** before assembly. The crumble will soften somewhat between cream layers overnight.

### 2. Prepare the coffee soak

1. Brew **200 ml strong espresso**.
2. While hot, stir in **2–3 g sugar** and **1–1.5 g cocoa** until dispersed.
3. Cool to approximately **40–50°C**, then add Marsala, Kahlúa and Grand Marnier.
4. Cool to **room temperature (approximately 20–25°C)** before dipping the biscuits.

### 3. Make the sabayon

1. Whisk **170–175 g yolks** with **160 g sugar** for **1–2 minutes** in a heatproof bowl.
2. Whisk in **15 ml Grand Marnier**.
3. Set the bowl over gently simmering water, ensuring its bottom does not touch the water.
3. Whisk continuously, including the sides and bottom, until the mixture is thick, pale, glossy and ribbon-like. This typically takes **6–10 minutes**, but texture and temperature matter more than time.
4. Aim for approximately **72–75°C**, measured in the egg mixture. Remove from heat promptly to avoid scrambling.
5. Whisk off heat briefly, add vanilla, and cool promptly to **20–25°C** (a cool-water bath helps).

> **Egg safety:** Briefly reaching 72–75°C does **not** guarantee pasteurization. Pasteurized yolks are the safer option, particularly for pregnant, elderly or immunocompromised guests.
{: .warn}

### 4. Combine the mascarpone and sabayon

1. Let the **1,000 g mascarpone** soften slightly while remaining cool, approximately **10–15°C**. Do not leave it at warm room temperature for an extended period.
2. Smooth the mascarpone gently with a spatula; avoid vigorous whipping.
3. Mix a small amount of cooled sabayon into the mascarpone to loosen it, then gently incorporate the rest in several additions.
4. Stop as soon as smooth and uniform.

### 5. Whip and fold in the cream

1. Whip **400 ml cold cream** with an electric mixer to **medium-soft peaks**: glossy peaks that bend at the tip.
2. Fold roughly one-third into the mascarpone mixture to lighten it.
3. Gently fold in the remaining two-thirds just until combined.

> **Note on this revision:** Increasing the cream from 300 ml to 400 ml produces more filling and a somewhat lighter, softer texture. It does **not** necessarily improve slice stability; avoid overwhipping and allow a full overnight chill.

### 6. Assemble

1. Quickly dip savoiardi in the room-temperature soak, approximately **½–1 second per side**, adjusting to their absorbency.
2. Arrange in one tight layer across the base of the tray. **Do not pour leftover soak over them.**
3. Spread approximately **45% of the mascarpone cream** over the biscuits.
4. Scatter a thin, even layer of fully cooled coffee crumble.
5. Cover with the remaining **55% of the cream** and smooth the top.
6. Cover and refrigerate at **4°C or colder for 12–16 hours**.
7. Dust with unsweetened cocoa **just before serving**.

## Temperatures

| Stage | Target |
|---|---|
| Sabayon cooking | 72–75°C, plus thick ribbon texture |
| Sabayon before mascarpone | 20–25°C |
| Mascarpone during mixing | ~10–15°C |
| Whipping cream | Cold, ~4°C |
| Coffee before adding alcohol | ~40–50°C |
| Coffee for dipping | Room temperature, ~20–25°C |
| Refrigeration | 4°C or colder |

## Notes

- This recipe uses **one savoiardi layer**, not two.
- The **200 ml espresso** is the coffee component, not the final volume after adding alcohol.
- Since tray size and biscuit absorbency vary, the quantity of savoiardi and amount of soak used are intentionally flexible.
